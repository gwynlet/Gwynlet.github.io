## The Sun

### Gwynlet

The Sun shone over the decrepit land like any other day, for it is its unrelenting duty. Does the Sun care for the land or does it simply exercise its task? We can't be really sure, for the Sun's existence outdates all of our books; we didn't have languages when it appeared, we didn't even have human bodies yet.

Since we can't ask the Sun directly what it thinks of us, we could maybe ask some intermediary; we could ask the sky, its blue infinity might have an answer to calm our troubled minds. Although I have never tried initiating conversation with it, I'm sure it's an utterly titanic task; the sky is cyclopean and we can't be certain if it is even sentient, so maybe it is not the best conversation partner. How about having a chat with the Moon? It must know about us and maintain relation with our shiny celestial golden king; poets have written about them together for aeons, painters have pictured them connected and lovers have dedicated the Moon to their partners since ever, they must have an intimate relation. Thinking about it, maybe not; the Moon is too beautiful and distant for us to interact with her, besides, people have talked about ripping her from the night vault to their loved ones for so many centuries that she's probably tired of feeling objectified, we don't want to bother her. The stars! That's it! The Sun is one of them, it may be the brightest and warmest, but still one of them, they must know their kin. Come to think of it, there are too many stars, how could we know which knows the Sun enough to know its feelings towards us? We could ask millions of them and never get an answer, do you not know someone with relatives that they don't know of? Why would the stars' kinship be any different?

How come the Sun never cared to speak to us? Is it because we are too many? Maybe its language is different to ours, maybe it speaks in old, dead tongues pre dating our existence; and, who do we think we are? Demanding words from the undying legendary star like we are worthy of hearing them, we have strayed from the path of its light and now think ourselves equals to it. I can't recall people worshipping it in the late times, no prayers were oralized nor people sacrificed in its name. Maybe we are just demanding too much from the Sun, maybe its warm, gentle touch is all we need.

A new thought has risen in my untiringly interested mind, why do we need confirmation of the Sun's love? Is it not enough that it shines on us and gives us cosmic energy throughout the whole day? Why do we feel the need to ask it if it cares for us while it gives us all he has constantly? It is light and warm, and that is what we receive of it; what else could we ask from it, if that is all it is? Even more, why do we assume it is its duty to shine on us? Does he even have a saying in that? Maybe he is tired of doing it and is still at it because of us! I think we should just enjoy the sun for what it is, instead of trying to analyze its motivations, and if nobody else wants to accompany me, I'll be grateful for its light every second I'm laying under its beautiful, golden rays.

I even think that, maybe, we are thinking too hard about a star casually burning the exact distance away from us to grant us life, instead of taking it; maybe we should simply be grateful for currently having physical bodies to feel its warmth because, for all we know, we may not have them anymore in the future. Maybe then we will be able only to gaze at its magnificent light, or maybe, not even that.
